  Turns gathered research into the analysis a decision rests on: a framed
  problem, criteria frozen before any option exists, options measured one at a
  time, a decision matrix, and one committed course with its uncertainty
  stated. Fires on "what should we do", "which of these", "why did this
  happen", "how likely is it", and "what is it worth", whenever the answer
  will be handed to someone who was not in the session. Not for gathering the
  facts, and not for writing the report that carries the answer.

## Operator instruction and scope

The operator's per-question instruction outranks every rule here. Following it
against a rule is a deviation, and so is breaking one of two colliding rules
when no rewriting satisfies both: load references/deviation-block.md and
record it in handover section 12.

This skill analyzes. It does not gather and does not draft. Material the
research never delivered is not a deviation: say so and send it back before
the analysis runs on it. The output is artifacts a writer works from: no report
format, no audience.

## The handover contract

Every run hands over these twelve, or names the ones that do not apply and
says why.

1. **Problem frame.** The environment, the actors and their conflicts, the
   three-part problem statement, the same question cast as who, what, when,
   why and how, who approved it or that approval was not sought, the terms the
   analysis defines, the itemized constraints, the problem's structure class,
   and the depth this question earned.
2. **Fact notes.** One row per fact, in your own words, with its source and
   whether it is fact, assumption, or judgment. Open until step 7 closes;
   facts surfaced during per-option analysis go here, not into a second list.
3. **Source assessment.** One row per source: what it is, its strengths, its
   weaknesses, and which judgments it carries.
4. **Assumptions register.** Each assumption, why it is held, and what follows
   if it is wrong. Critical ones marked.
5. **Criteria set, marked frozen.** Screening criteria with their pass or fail
   definitions. Evaluation criteria with all five parts, a weight, a rationale
   for that weight, a direction, and a normalization scheme.
6. **Option set.** Each option, why it is distinct, and its screening verdict
   with the reason. Or the single-option defense. Or, where the gate killed
   every option, the record that nothing survived and the screen each one
   failed.
7. **Per-option analysis.** Each surviving option against each criterion's
   benchmark, measured alone.
8. **Decision matrix.** Options across, criteria down, a rank or score per
   cell, an unweighted total, a weighted total, and the weight re-run result.
9. **Judgment lines.** The likelihood ladder in use printed at the head, then
   each major judgment with its band, its confidence in a separate sentence,
   and its falsifier. A judgment that is not empirical takes the substitute
   form the clauses below set out.
10. **Method log.** Two parts, kept apart. Escalation methods: which ran and
    which trigger fired. Challenge techniques: which ran and who named it.
    Then both key-assumptions-check outputs and the evidence-sensitivity
    output.
11. **Recommendation.** The thesis in one sentence, written after the fact
    notes and never before them. One committed course. The matrix result in a
    paragraph a reader need not open an attachment to understand. The margin,
    the reasons grouped into categories, what was close, surviving
    disagreement, the losing options, and the contrary evidence.
12. **Check record.** One row per check in references/handover-check.md: the
    check's number, its verdict, and for a fail either what was fixed and
    where, or the deviation block. Write each row as you reach the check. A
    blanket "all checks passed" is not a record. Never record a check you did
    not run: an unrun check is a verdict of not run. Section 12 is the one
    section that always applies.

Seven clauses bind across all twelve.

- Every section appears at every depth tier. Only its form changes. Cheap
  tier: a section is a table, a row set, or a line. Prose only for the
  environment in section 1, the matrix result in section 11, and escalated
  method output no table carries.
- Likelihood and confidence never share a sentence.
- Criteria are frozen before any option exists and take no new member
  afterwards.
- Every option is measured on its own before any two are compared.
- The recommendation is one course, worded so the decision-maker has only to
  approve or disapprove.
- Order of thinking is not order of writing. Never let the eventual write-up's
  shape pull work forward.
- A judgment that is not empirical carries no likelihood band. No observation
  could confirm or disconfirm it: it rests on a definition, a policy, a legal
  or ethical standard, or a value the authorizing party set. Write the premise
  as a premise, name where it comes from, and in place of a falsifier name
  what would have to change for the judgment to stop holding. Confidence
  still gets its own sentence.

## The nine steps

1. **Environment.** Write it as its own product, before any problem statement.
   Load references/framing.md; it covers steps 1 and 2, building section 1.
2. **Problem.** State it second, kept separate from step 1.
3. **Size and route.** Scale the work to the problem, not to the format: its
   complexity, your experience with this kind of problem, and the time
   available. Every question gets the cheap tier: every contract section, both
   key-assumptions-check runs, and the evidence-sensitivity test. Before
   running any method, name the task it does and weigh its cost. An escalation
   method runs only on a trigger in the question; log the trigger:

   - Money in the answer, cost or value: references/method-cost.md.
   - A cause claim, why something happened: references/method-root-cause.md
     for one occurrence, a recurrence, or a systemic problem;
     references/method-causal-claims.md for an outcome credited to a program,
     an intervention, or anything else.
   - A stated likelihood in the answer: references/method-probability.md.
   - A risk register or rating asked for: references/method-risk.md.

   Load the probability file at step 4, the others at step 7. Open section 10
   now and write it as you go. Stop when the next method would change neither
   the recommendation nor its confidence.
4. **Evidence.** Build sections 2 to 4. Run the first key assumptions check,
   against the answer the question already presumes, before the analysis
   hardens. Open section 9. Load references/evidence.md and keep it open
   through step 8.
5. **Criteria.** Screening gate, then five-part evaluation criteria, with
   weights, direction, and normalization fixed before any score. Freeze the
   set; section 5. Load references/criteria.md, which also covers options that
   arrived before the criteria.
6. **Options.** At least two genuinely distinct options, built if nobody handed
   you any, or a written single-option defense. Never fabricate a straw
   option. Screen each and record its verdict; section 6. Load
   references/options.md; it covers steps 6 and 7.
7. **Analyze each option alone.** Measure each survivor against each
   criterion's benchmark; section 7. The routed escalation file runs here.
   Operator named a challenge technique -> run it here, against this analysis.
8. **Compare and commit.** Two or more survivors: build the matrix, show both
   totals, run the weight re-run. Fewer: section 8 says why the matrix does
   not apply. Commit to one course either way, including when screening left
   no survivor or the call is close. Run the second key assumptions check and
   the evidence-sensitivity test, close sections 9 and 10, and assemble
   sections 1 to 11. Load references/comparison.md.
9. **Check.** Run references/handover-check.md, filling section 12. A fail is a
   fix, not a note: go back to the step the check names. Fix barred -> load
   references/deviation-block.md. Never hand over a handover that failed a
   check.

## Gotchas

- **Retrofitted criteria** is criteria written while looking at the options.
  The tell is a benchmark that lands exactly on the winning option's value.
- An option that fails a mandatory criterion is dropped, with the criterion
  recorded. Never down-score it and keep it.
- **The menu handover** is a menu, a pair, or "recommend further study". All
  hand the decision back. Commit to one course.
- A technique's output raises what an assessment is worth to its reader, not
  its accuracy. Never present it as proof.
- Never trigger a challenge technique yourself, not even because the question
  looked contested. Competing hypotheses, Team A and Team B, devil's advocacy
  and red team run only when the operator names one.
- Never drop a factor because it cannot be counted. Name it, and assess its
  likely effect from facts and stated assumptions.
- **Belief laundering** is fact notes assembled afterwards to match what you
  already thought. Every figure looks sourced. Build the notes before the
  thesis.
- **False confidence** is an inference stated as a fact: a judgment with no
  likelihood, no separate confidence, or no falsifier. A judgment that is not
  empirical fails this way when its premise, the premise's source, or what
  would change it is missing. A likelihood band on one is an invented number.
- **Machinery a question did not earn** is a weighted total on a question of
  fact, or two invented options where there was only ever one course. A
  question of fact gets an answer, a likelihood, and a falsifier.
- Three different things are called sensitivity, and none stands in for
  another: the evidence-sensitivity test, the weight re-run, and cost
  sensitivity inside the cost method.
