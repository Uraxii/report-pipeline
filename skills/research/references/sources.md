# Sources: what each kind hides, and how to rate one

## What each class withholds

Ask of a candidate what it would never tell you, not whether it is true.

- **The subject's own site or brochure.** Good for current positioning and what
  it wants known. Withholds anything it would rather you not know. Never rest a
  claim on it alone.
- **A self-supplied profile or directory entry.** Good for names, dates, and
  claimed scope. Withholds anything unflattering. It is not verification.
- **A mandatory filing or disclosure.** Good for audited figures, pending
  litigation, and obligations the subject would not volunteer. Withholds
  motive and anything outside its schedule.
- **A first-party operational record.** Good for what actually happened inside
  that system. Withholds whatever the system does not instrument, and that
  silence reads as absence.
- **An independent investigation or trade write-up.** Good for judgment on
  quality, culture, and how an organization treats people. Withholds internals
  it could not reach, and carries the writer's angle.
- **A regulator's or auditor's report.** Good for a standard applied by
  somebody with no stake in the answer. Old by the time you read it.
- **A person who was there.** Good for motive, sequence, and what the record
  leaves out. Weak on numbers, dates, and anything they inferred rather than
  saw.

Take subjective matters from writing the subject did not control. Take what
the subject would rather hide from what it is compelled to publish. Take
sequence and motive from people who were there.

## Rate the document on two axes, never one

- **Originator reliability.** How far this body's output has held up in the
  past, independent of this document.
- **Document credibility.** How far this document is to be trusted on its own.
  A reliable originator publishes an early estimate. A poor originator
  publishes a verbatim court transcript.

Use a letter for originator reliability and a separate numeral for document
credibility, on scales stated at the top of the table. Each scale has a value
meaning "cannot be judged", which is not "poor". Never average the two into
one value.

Originator's reliability unknown: say unknown. Do not fully accept or fully
reject the document. Carry the uncertainty in its row. Write down the standard
you used to judge reliability and mark it as your own.

## Corroboration

Two documents agreeing count only if they could have disagreed.

Signs of circular reporting: identical figures to identical precision, the
same unusual phrasing, the same error, a citation chain that converges.

For a claim critical to the eventual answer, say in its row whether a document
deriving from something else corroborates it, and say plainly when none does.
